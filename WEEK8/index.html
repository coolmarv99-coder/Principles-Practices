Opening vignette

“What Fits This Child?”

Jordan: “I think I’m starting to understand why we keep circling back to child development. It’s not just background information.”

Maya: “Right. If we understand development, we make better choices. We don’t expect toddlers to sit like third graders, and we don’t assume every four-year-old should be doing the exact same thing at the exact same time.”

Aaliyah: “And development is not just one category. Physical, cognitive, social, and emotional development overlap.”

Jordan: “So developmentally appropriate practice is basically asking, ‘What makes sense for this age, this child, this family, and this situation?’”

Maya: “That sounds simple, but it takes a lot of observation and judgment.”

Aaliyah: “And humility. We have to know child development, but we also have to pay attention to the individual child in front of us.”

Jordan: “So halfway through the course, maybe the question is changing. It’s not just, ‘What should teachers do?’ It’s, ‘What fits this child right now?’”

Think first: How can teachers balance knowledge of typical development with respect for each child’s abilities, culture, experiences, and needs?
